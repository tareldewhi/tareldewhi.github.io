<h1>The Human Message</h1>

<p>The Human Message addresses two related questions:</p>

<div class="hm-questions">
  <p class="hm-question">
    What should a message contain in order to represent the human species?
  </p>

  <p class="hm-question">
    And what characteristics should the message itself possess in order to be
    recognized, interpreted and answered by another technological species?
  </p>
</div>

<p>
 The first question — explored for decades through a variety of interstellar messaging projects, from the Arecibo message onward — still appeared to me the more elusive and difficult to answer. So I started with the second one.
</p>

<p>
  The second question seemed more promising, because I could define the ideal
  characteristics of such a message regardless of whether they were technically
  feasible or not. I identified four:
</p>

<div class="hm-message-grid">

  <div class="hm-message-item">
    <h3>Representative</h3>
    <p>
      It should represent humanity across a very broad space-time continuum,
      ideally encompassing the entire existence of the species rather than
      a single culture or historical period.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="hm-message-item">
    <h3>Self-contained</h3>
    <p>
      It should provide meaningful information about humanity even if no further
      interaction takes place.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="hm-message-item">
    <h3>Interactive</h3>
    <p>
      It should allow an unknown technological species to recognize that
      <strong>the message is a message</strong> and make a coherent response possible.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="hm-message-item">
    <h3>Capable of sustaining a silent conversation</h3>
    <p>
      Communication should not end with a single transmission. Each interaction
      should be capable of generating new information from what has already
      been exchanged.
    </p>
  </div>

</div>

<hr class="hm-divider">
<br>
<p>
  Once I had clearly in mind that an effective message should be
  <strong>representative, self-contained, interactive and capable of generating
  new information through each interaction</strong>, I could return to the first question.
</p>

<p>And this immediately raised another one:</p>

<p class="hm-question">
  What is the human species? In a nutshell, what defines us as a species?
</p>

<h2>A biobehavioral model of humanity</h2>

<p>
  Rather than representing humanity through particular technologies, cultures
  or historical events, I developed a working model based on
  <strong>six core biobehavioral characteristics and one fundamental dynamic relationship</strong>.
</p>

<p>
  The model was developed first and subsequently compared with research from several
  disciplines in order to test and support its underlying assumptions.
</p>

<div class="hm-characteristics">

  <div class="hm-characteristic">
    <h3>1. Social species</h3>
    <p>
      Humans are organized in small groups of interdependent individuals,
      providing the biological and behavioral basis for both
      <strong>cooperation and conflict</strong>.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="hm-characteristic">
    <h3>2. Modification of the environment</h3>
    <p>
      Humans modify — often radically — and adapt natural environments to their needs,
      leading toward what I call
      <strong>Urbanized Dominating Ecosystems</strong>.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="hm-characteristic">
    <h3>3. Tools as body augmentation</h3>
    <p>
      Humans use tools to extend the capabilities of the body, creating a continuous
      path from elementary implements to
      <strong>technology and machines</strong>.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="hm-characteristic">
    <h3>4. Emotional interpretation of reality</h3>
    <p>
      Human perception, imagination and interpretation are strongly shaped by basic
      emotions such as fear, grief, love and rage. Reality is not simply observed:
      it is continuously interpreted. This is one of the paths leading toward
      <strong>storytelling</strong>.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="hm-characteristic">
    <h3>5. Architecture mindset</h3>
    <p>
      Human thought tends to organize reality through objects, functions, relationships
      and structures. Experiences can therefore be decomposed, recombined and represented
      as structured mental models. This provides another path toward
      <strong>storytelling</strong>.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="hm-characteristic hm-characteristic--relation">
    <h3>6. The interaction between emotion and structure</h3>
    <p>
      Characteristics 4 and 5 operate together:
      <strong>emotion assigns meaning and relevance, while architectural thought
      gives experience structure</strong>. Their interaction transforms experience
      into representations that can be remembered, communicated and reinterpreted.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="hm-characteristic hm-characteristic--storytelling">
    <h3>7. Storytelling</h3>
    <p>
      Humans organize experiences, actors, intentions, causes and possibilities
      into communicable structures. Storytelling contributes to the emergence of
      <strong>society, politics, religion, art, exploration and science</strong>,
      expressed differently across human cultures.
    </p>
  </div>

</div>

<h2>Urbanized Dominating Ecosystems</h2>

<p>
  I use the term <strong>Urbanized Dominating Ecosystems</strong> to describe environments
  extensively transformed around human needs. <em>Urbanized</em> does not simply mean
  <em>urban</em>: cities, villages, cultivated fields, roads, gardens and managed landscapes
  are all different expressions of ecosystems reorganized by human activity.
</p>

<p>
  Human modification is also perceptual. Our environments are filled with visual,
  acoustic, olfactory and symbolic signals that influence behavior and the way we
  experience space. Other species do this too: a lion's roar can instantly alter
  another animal's perception of its surroundings. Human environments differ,
  however, in the <strong>scale and complexity</strong> of this process.
</p>

<p>
  I call this informational and perceptual layer the
  <strong>perceptive ecosystem</strong>: humans modify their environments, and those
  modified environments continuously influence human perception and behavior in return.
</p>
